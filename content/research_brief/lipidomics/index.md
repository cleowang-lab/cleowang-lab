Lipids are fundamental biomolecules that play critical roles in maintaining cellular structure, energy storage, and signaling. Beyond their structural and metabolic functions, lipids are key regulators of diverse biological processes, including membrane dynamics, vesicle trafficking, inflammation, and cell death. Dysregulation of lipid metabolism is increasingly recognized as a hallmark of numerous diseases, such as neurodegenerative disorders, metabolic syndromes, and cancer.
Our research focuses on systematically characterizing the lipid landscape and understanding how lipid composition, distribution, and interactions change under physiological and pathological conditions. We employ state-of-the-art mass spectrometry–based lipidomics, in combination with advanced separation technologies, chemical labeling strategies, and integrative bioinformatics analysis, to achieve comprehensive and quantitative profiling of lipid species. Furthermore, we explore how lipids interact with proteins and other biomolecules to modulate cellular signaling and function.
Ultimately, our goal is to link lipid alterations to disease mechanisms, identify novel lipid biomarkers, and uncover therapeutic targets. By advancing the field of lipidomics, our research seeks to provide deeper insights into lipid biology and its implications for human health and disease.
