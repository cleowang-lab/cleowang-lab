Aging is a fundamental biological process marked by the progressive decline of cellular and physiological functions, leading to increased vulnerability to diseases such as Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and Parkinson’s disease (PD). Our research aims to uncover the molecular mechanisms underlying aging and neurodegeneration, with a particular focus on how protein–lipid interactions, vesicle dynamics, and membrane biology contribute to neuronal aging and dysfunction. By combining in situ structural biology, mass spectrometry–based lipidomics, and cellular and animal models, we seek to understand how lipid signaling and vesicle trafficking change during aging and how these changes influence disease progression. Furthermore, we aim to identify biomarkers and therapeutic targets by integrating molecular data with disease phenotypes. Ultimately, our goal is to translate these mechanistic insights into novel strategies for early diagnosis, intervention, and treatment of aging-related neurodegenerative disorders, thereby contributing to healthier aging and improved quality of life in the elderly population.
